Terry A. Davis

1969 – 2018

He wrote an operating system, a compiler, a graphics stack, a document format and a language. Alone, over about ten years, and he made every one of them follow from a single stated idea.

I think he was making art with it. I am not certain he would have said so, and I am not certain I am right. What I am sure of is that nobody has made anything like it before or since, and that the coherence — the way one decision reaches all the way down to the last line of it — is rarer than the code and harder than the code.

He was ill, and that is in the work, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of disrespect. It is also not the interesting part, and it is not mine to talk about.

I miss the things he never got to make.

His family asked that people who want to mark his death give to organisations working on mental illness. They named the Brain & Behavior Research Foundation and the National Alliance on Mental Illness.

This page is here because he is worth remembering, and for no other reason. Nether C is not about him and does not need him to justify it. Where his work is genuinely load-bearing — the palette is derived from VGA, and the arguments about constraint are argued out in §90.2 — it is cited there, like any other source.
